ARENA: Active Region attENtion Aggregation for interpretable solar flare prediction

Authors: Luis Bauer, Annalena Kofler, Andrés Asensio Ramos, Aparna Venkataramanasastry, Valentín Martínez Pillet, Bernhard Schölkopf
Comments: 7+12 pages (2+8 figures), accepted at the "Representations for the Physical Sciences" workshop at NeurIPS 2026
Primary Category: astro-ph.SR
All Categories: astro-ph.SR, astro-ph.IM, cs.CV, cs.LG

Predicting solar flares is a challenging space-weather forecasting problem with potentially significant impacts on Earth's upper atmosphere, including disruptions to radio communications. We propose Active Region attENtion Aggregation (ARENA), a neural network architecture that predicts solar flares from a variable number of active-region cutouts while learning to identify the region most relevant to the prediction. ARENA is trained using multiple-instance learning under weak supervision, requiring only a flare/no-flare label for the observation and no labels specifying the flare origin. The learned attention mechanism therefore provides a means of localizing the active region driving the prediction without explicit origin annotations. ARENA achieves a heidke skill score of 0.45 which is comparable to other flare prediction approaches. These results demonstrate that active-region-based aggregation can provide an efficient and interpretable approach to solar flare prediction. The code is available at https://github.com/LuisBauer22/ARENA.


SoFTg: A hybrid segmentation framework for solar granulation revealing turbulent convection statistics at DKIST scales

Authors: M. Berretti, S. Criscuoli, A. Fanti, P. H. Keys, L. Bertello, M. Stangalini, F. Berrilli
Comments: Accepted on ApJS. Movies available on the publisher website
Primary Category: astro-ph.SR
All Categories: astro-ph.SR, astro-ph.IM, physics.data-an

In this work, we present Solar Feature Tracking: granules (SoFTg), a new hybrid algorithm within the SoFT library designed for the segmentation and tracking of solar granulation. By combining a heuristic approach based on a custom adaptive-thresholding technique with the speed and robustness of deep-learning methods, SoFTg provides a fast, reliable and user-friendly solution for granule segmentation, followed by the standard SoFT approach for tracking. We present a comprehensive evaluation of the algorithm's performance using multiple metrics and analyse the different stages of the processing pipeline. The method is tested on three datasets acquired with the Visible Broadband Imager operating in the red continuum at the Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope in Maui, Hawaii. We present distributions of both the lifetime and equivalent diameter of the thousands of granules tracked, providing, for the first time, statistically robust evidence of turbulent convection at the spatial scales now accessible through the high-resolution imaging capabilities of DKIST.


Machine Learning Applications in the Solar Interior and Atmosphere

Authors: Vishal Upendran, Carlos J. Díaz Baso
Comments: Review chapter submitted to the book Machine Learning Techniques for Astrophysics and Cosmology by Spring Singapore
Primary Category: astro-ph.SR
All Categories: astro-ph.SR, astro-ph.IM, cs.LG

The solar interior and atmosphere constitute a strongly coupled, multiscale dynamical system defined by continuous temporal evolution. Predicting the state of this system requires modeling physical interactions that span disparate spatial and temporal scales, constrained by heterogeneous and sparse data. This chapter formalizes the Sun within the mathematical framework of dynamical systems theory, defining a global state vector ($\vec{S}$) governed by thermodynamical, kinetic, and magnetic properties. Because solar observations represent projected, multi-modal, and partially observable measurements ($\vec{O}[\vec{S}]$) of this hidden state, pure physics-based deduction is fundamentally constrained. We present a unifying thesis: the primary role of machine learning in heliophysics is to act as a structured family of mathematical operators---specifically Inference ($\mathcal{I}$), Emulation ($\mathcal{E}$), Propagation ($\mathcal{P}$), and Control ($\mathcal{C}$)---that reconstruct, simulate, and couple this global state vector across disparate physical layers. By framing the vast, multi-disciplinary literature within this unified state-space taxonomy, we demonstrate how data-driven techniques bridge observational gaps to resolve hidden multiscale couplings and dramatically accelerate the simulation of non-linear processes, establishing a rigorous pathway to predict the global evolution of the Sun.


Distilling neural networks into equations to predict planetary instability

Authors: Simon Alford, Edward Gu, Omar Costilla-Reyes, Dan Tamayo, Miles Cranmer, Kevin Ellis
Comments: 32 pages, 15 figures, 3 tables. Code and data: https://github.com/simonalford42/planet_eqs
Primary Category: cs.LG
All Categories: cs.LG, astro-ph.EP

The problem of predicting when a multi-planet system will become dynamically unstable has defied analytical progress over the past three hundred years. Recent machine learning advances achieve high predictive performance of instability dynamics, but as black-box systems cannot be interpreted to connect to existing scientific understanding. Here we show analytic equations that predict when a multi-planet system will undergo instability. We discover these equations by first training a neural network on a dataset of N-body simulations, and then distilling its latent knowledge into closed-form algebraic expressions. Our two-stage pipeline first learns an interpretable variable basis, then searches for equations using symbolic regression. The resulting equations outperform existing analytic estimates of instability and apply across wide ranges of mass ratios, spacing, and orbital eccentricities. They capture structure in the prediction landscape with a small number of variables amenable to dynamical interpretation. These results demonstrate how symbolic learning can be combined with neural networks to extract scientific insight from complex dynamical systems.


Near-exact Upper Confidence Limits for Axion Haloscopes

Authors: Andrea Gallo Rosso, Jan Conrad
Comments: 15 pages, 3 figures
Primary Category: physics.data-an
All Categories: physics.data-an, astro-ph.IM, hep-ex, hep-ph

The data acquisition of axion haloscopes commonly implements sequential adaptive frequency scans. We present a method to calculate likelihood-based upper confidence limits on axion-photon coupling under this circumstance. We compare the likelihood-based method conceptually and quantitatively with tailor-made methods, i.e. the one proposed by Brubaker et al. (Geometric) and its upgrade the Bayesian Power-Measured (BPM) framework proposed by Palken et al. We find that the likelihood method yields average upper limits that are significantly more constraining than those obtained from these methods. For example, expected constraints improve by a factor of 1.4 as compared with the current state of the art when applied to projections for the ALPHA Plasma-haloscope. We further clarify the statistical basis for the Geometric and BPM methods. In particular, we point out the correspondence of BPM to e-values, proposed as an alternative to conventional p-values in statistics.


Explainable Prediction of High-Speed Solar Wind Streams driven by Coronal Holes via Topological Data Analysis

Authors: Francesco Marchetti, Sabrina Guastavino, Daniel Collin
Comments: No comment found
Primary Category: astro-ph.SR
All Categories: astro-ph.SR, cs.LG

High-speed solar wind streams (HSSs) originating from coronal holes (CHs) are an important driver of recurrent geomagnetic activity. Since HSSs are associated to coronal structures visible in extreme-ultraviolet observations, this provides a natural basis for forecasting several days in advance. In this work, we investigate whether the topology of coronal structures observed in extreme ultraviolet (EUV) images contains predictive information on the subsequent occurrence of CH-driven HSSs at Earth. To this end, we consider Topological Data Analysis (TDA), and in particular persistent homology, to classify EUV solar images according to the occurrence of HSSs at Earth four days later. The target is a binary classification of HSS occurrence at a four-day lead time, defined using an HSS interval catalog and solar wind speed thresholds. Several combinations of topological representations and classifiers are evaluated under a common cross-validation framework. The results indicate that the topology of EUV coronal structures alone contains predictive information on subsequent CH-driven HSS occurrence, despite the absence of explicit CH geometric descriptors in the model input. To investigate the origin of this predictive information, we develop a model-level explainability framework based on cross-validation-aggregated feature weights and spatial evidence maps. A fully traced example connects persistence diagrams and persistence-image features to the classifier output and to their spatial localization in the original EUV observation. The proposed framework provides a transparent and computationally lightweight approach for investigating the relationship between coronal topology and subsequent high-speed solar wind streams.


Automated, auditable dating of the Babylonian Astronomical Diaries with language models and astronomical tools

Authors: Lucas Carrit Delgado Pinheiro, Yuan-Sen Ting, Jon Clindaniel, Lu Li, Yingbin Liang, Ness Shroff
Comments: 15 pages, 6 figures, 7 tables, including the SI Appendix. Code, data and interactive page: https://github.com/tingyuansen/Babylonian_Dating and https://tingyuansen.github.io/Babylonian_Dating/
Primary Category: astro-ph.IM
All Categories: astro-ph.IM, cs.AI, cs.CL, physics.hist-ph

The Babylonian Astronomical Diaries record the sky over nearly six centuries. Many tablets are broken and have lost their date formula. Experts dated many of them one tablet at a time in the standard edition, Astronomical Diaries and Related Texts from Babylonia (ADART). They used the recorded sky and other evidence, such as year formulas and royal names. Our system automates the astronomical part for all tablets with the same rules. A rule-based parser turns the recorded observations into clauses, short statements with fixed fields. A language model checks them against the text and adds observations that the parser missed. Astronomical tools test the clauses in all 701 years from 626 BCE to AD 75. The system ranks the years by how well they agree with the clauses. The system withholds published dates until its results are final, and the output lists the passages and computed positions behind each candidate year. On 120 dated tablets, the years tied at the top of the ranking include the published year in 96% of cases. For 86 of them, this top group is a single year, the published year in 84. In 119 of the 120, the reported candidate set contains it. For 56 undated entries of ADART IV, the system gives ranked candidate years. For each tablet, the results show how far astronomy alone narrows the year. An interactive page at https://tingyuansen.github.io/Babylonian_Dating/ follows each tablet and links the code, protocol and results.
