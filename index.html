HLA-Net: A Physics-Guided Skeleton-Based Framework for Automatic Gravitational Arc Detection in Astronomical Images

Authors: Don S
Comments: 16 pages, 2 figures, planning to submit: The Open Journal of Astrophysics
Primary Category: astro-ph.IM
All Categories: astro-ph.IM, astro-ph.GA, cs.LG

Gravitational arcs produced by strong gravitational lensing provide a unique approach for investigating dark matter distribution, galaxy cluster mass reconstruction and the structure of the Universe. Automated detection remains challenging because gravitational arcs are rare, often faint and morphologically similar to elongated galaxies.In this paper, we propose \textbf{HLA-Net (Hybrid Lens Arc Network)}, an interpretable multi-stage framework for automatic gravitational arc detection that explicitly exploits both the geometric morphology and physical characteristics of gravitational lensing without requiring manually annotated training datasets.The proposed framework was evaluated on astronomical FITS images containing complex galaxy cluster structures. Starting from 169 initial arc candidates extracted from ordered skeleton representations, the multi-stage verification pipeline reduced the search space to 83 geometrically consistent candidates, while robust circle fitting successfully modelled 113 candidate arc structures. The results demonstrate that HLA-Net effectively suppresses false-positive detections while preserving physically reasonable lens candidates through an interpretable and modular verification strategy.


Recovering Missing Degrees of Freedom in Simulation-Trained Field-Level Generative Models

Authors: Masato Shirasaki, Shiro Ikeda
Comments: 4+14 pages, 1+8 figures
Primary Category: astro-ph.CO
All Categories: astro-ph.CO, cs.LG

Simulation-trained generative models are emerging as a powerful framework for field-level inference in cosmology, particularly in strongly nonlinear regimes where analytical approaches become intractable. However, the finite size of training datasets often leads to variance shrinkage, indicating that part of the realization-level variability of the underlying physical process is not captured by the learned generative dynamics. In this Letter, we interpret variance shrinkage as a diagnostic of missing effective degrees of freedom and introduce physics-informed latent modifications to the generative dynamics of a continuous normalizing flow. We show that these latent corrections recover a substantial fraction of the missing variability without requiring additional training simulations. Using weak-lensing field-level inference as a representative cosmological application, we further demonstrate through posterior analysis that the learned latent directions are actively exploited during inference and behave as effective nuisance parameters that absorb part of the mismatch between the training and target distributions. As a result, the model exhibits improved robustness to out-of-distribution shifts in field-level inference. Our work establishes variance shrinkage as a practical diagnostic for identifying missing degrees of freedom in simulation-trained generative models and provides a framework for recovering them through physics-informed modifications of generative dynamics.


Anomaly-ranked confirmation slows new-class learning

Authors: Argyro Sasli, Michael Coughlin
Comments: 9 pages, 4 figures
Primary Category: astro-ph.IM
All Categories: astro-ph.IM, cs.LG, physics.data-an

Anomaly detectors rank unfamiliar examples for expert confirmation, and the confirmed examples teach a classifier to recognise a new class. Across five benchmarks from physics, medicine and computer vision, the members flagged by the detector are those the classifier learns first. In three systems, confirming the highest-ranked members of a new class gives lower recognition than confirming the same number of random members. Handing subsequent selection to the promoted classifier recovers part of this loss in two systems. A paired intervention further shows that replacing nearby known classes with distant ones sharply reduces the confirmations needed for recognition. These results distinguish finding a new class from selecting examples that teach its extent: within the tested classes, concentrating confirmations at the top of the anomaly ranking makes learning less efficient.


Learned Surrogates of Three-Dimensional Magnetohydrodynamic Turbulence

Authors: Semih Kacmaz, E. A. Huerta
Comments: 25 pages, 7 figures, 12 tables
Primary Category: cs.LG
All Categories: cs.LG, astro-ph.IM, physics.flu-dyn, physics.plasm-ph

Three-dimensional magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) turbulence, the flow of astrophysical and fusion plasmas, is costly to simulate. We present, to our knowledge, the first learned surrogates of three-dimensional resistive MHD turbulence, and compare three standard backbones in that role: a convolutional UNet, a shifted-window transformer and a Fourier neural operator. They are trained as autoregressive emulators of decaying MHD turbulence at a resolution of $128^3$ and Reynolds numbers of 1000, 3000 and 6000, on 500 simulations per Reynolds number, each with one two-stage procedure, single steps and then short rollouts, and stopped by one rule. Over a window of about one large-eddy turnover time, the UNet and the transformer reach relative velocity errors of 0.018 to 0.053 and keep the current sheets, the enstrophy, the current density and the energy spectra of the simulation; which of the two is the more accurate depends on the Reynolds number, the UNet leading at 3000 and 6000 and the transformer at 1000. Applied with the same backbones and training procedure to two public benchmarks, the models reach the lowest error reported to date on the isotropic turbulence set of FactFormer, 39% below the best published model. On the compressible MHD set of The Well, their rollout errors are a third to a half below those of the best of The Well's baselines. They also match a 1.3 B-parameter pretrained foundation model in its rollout windows, with 20-62 times fewer parameters. The code, the configuration of every model, the evaluation scripts and the data generator with the seed of every simulation will be released upon publication.


Gravitational-Wave Microlensing as a Probe of Compact Object Population

Authors: Eungwang Seo, Jose María Ezquiaga, Martin Hendry
Comments: 17 pages, 7 figures, 2 tables
Primary Category: astro-ph.CO
All Categories: astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, physics.data-an

The abundance of compact objects ranging from stellar to intermediate masses ($\sim 1 - 10^{5}M_{\odot}$) remains poorly constrained, particularly for populations such as primordial black holes and dark matter subhalos that emit little or no electromagnetic radiation. Gravitational-wave (GW) microlensing provides a complementary way to probe these populations through wave-optics effects imprinted on the GW signals, with implications for astrophysical and cosmological inference. We develop a hierarchical Bayesian framework to infer the population properties of microlenses from an ensemble of microlensed GW events, constraining the slope of the power-law lens mass function ($α_{\rm ML}$), the lens number density ($n_0$), and the fraction of dark matter in compact lenses ($f_{\rm DM}$). Using simulated microlensed GW signals drawn from a realistic binary black hole population, we find that $\sim 100$ detected microlensed events are able to constrain both $α_{\rm ML}$ and $n_0$, although a degeneracy between the two remains. For a low-mass-dominated lens population ($α_{\rm ML}=2$) with $n_0=10^6~\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$, a three-year observation at the O4 sensitivity yields $\sim 130$ detectable microlensed events and recovers the injected dark-matter fraction, $f_{\rm DM}\simeq 1.2 \times 10^{-4}$, with a 90% credible interval of $7.3 \times 10^{-5} \lesssim f_{\rm DM} \lesssim 1.8 \times 10^{-4}$.


3D dark matter map of the JWST COSMOS-Web field using diffusion-based generative priors

Authors: Diana Scognamiglio, Brandon Zhao, Katherine L. Bouman, Olivier Doré, Caitlin M. Casey, Maximilien Franco, Ghassem Gozaliasl, David Harvey, Olivier Ilbert, Jeffrey B. Jewell, Jeyhan S. Kartaltepe, Anton M. Koekemoer, Gavin Leroy, Richard Massey, Jason Rhodes, Brant E. Robertson, Marko Shuntov, Sune Toft, Greta Toni, Michael A. Troxel
Comments: 24 pages, 5 main figures, 6 extended data figures
Primary Category: astro-ph.CO
All Categories: astro-ph.CO, cs.LG

Mapping the three-dimensional distribution of dark matter is key to tracing the growth of cosmic structure. Two-dimensional weak lensing mass maps recover its projected distribution, but they blend structures at different distances along the line of sight. We reconstruct the three-dimensional matter distribution in the 0.54 deg$^2$ COSMOS-Web field using deep James Webb Space Telescope imaging, with an effective source density of 121 galaxies arcmin$^{-2}$ We combine a physical weak lensing forward model with a diffusion-based Bayesian generative prior learned from cosmological simulations, yielding a posterior distribution of the matter field across 20 lens planes to $z \simeq 2$, doubling the redshift reach of previous Hubble Space Telescope-based 3D reconstructions and approaching cosmic noon, an epoch of rapid galaxy growth. The reconstruction separates structures that overlap in projection and recovers non-Gaussian matter structure across a range of spatial scales, including over-densities associated with independently identified galaxy clusters that were not detected in earlier weak lensing maps. Posterior sampling quantifies the range of three-dimensional matter distributions allowed by the data, enabling a probabilistic visualization of the cosmic web while propagating uncertainties in its inferred structure. Null and simulation-based tests show that the recovered structures are supported by the lensing signal and are consistent with expectations from the fiducial $Λ$CDM cosmology. These results extend three-dimensional weak lensing mapping into the high-redshift, non-Gaussian regime and establish a route towards probabilistic mapping of the evolving cosmic web with current wide-field lensing surveys.


An automated photometric pipeline for measuring the corotation radius of spiral galaxies

Authors: Kimia Yazdani, Sahel Mesforoush, Wayne B. Hayes
Comments: No comment found
Primary Category: astro-ph.GA
All Categories: astro-ph.GA, cs.CV, physics.data-an

The corotation radius R_CR of a spiral galaxy marks the radius where the angular speed of the spiral pattern matches the orbital speed of the disk material. Despite its importance for understanding spiral structure and galaxy evolution, very few galaxies have corotation measurements, often derived using heterogeneous techniques. We present an automated photometric pipeline that identifies corotation radii by tracking the azimuthal phase offset between young and old stellar populations. Building on the color-gradient framework of \citet{Puerari1997}, we extract azimuthal brightness profiles from deprojected dual-band images, fit periodic models to the profiles, and identify corotation candidates at radii where the phase difference between the two stellar tracers changes sign. Each candidate is assigned an adaptive uncertainty interval and reported with a goodness-of-fit metric. We benchmark the method against a cross-method literature compilation, scoring every technique -- including our own -- against a reference built from the published measurements alone and evaluated leave-one-out. Over the $90$ galaxies for which such a reference exists, the pipeline places a correct corotation radius in $80.0\%$ of them, recovers $63.6\%$ of their known corotation radii, and reaches a harmonic mean of recall and purity of $0.58$, the highest values of any method in the comparison. Unlike the published methods, each of which was applied to a small, curated set of galaxies, our pipeline is run on the entire reference sample without any pre-selection, a sample two to six times larger than existing methods. The pipeline is scalable to large surveys, flexible with respect to spiral mode, and naturally recovers multiple corotation radii in galaxies with complex spiral structure, providing a uniform framework for studying disk resonances across diverse galaxy populations.


Feature-Based and Deep Learning Methods for Astronomical Light Curve Classification

Authors: V. Baymakanov, Kh. Khamitov, A. Raimova, A. Isaeva, R. Taran, M. Zedan, M. Kornilov, M. Demianenko, K. Malanchev, M. Hushchyn
Comments: 14 pages, 5 figures. Submitted to Moscow University Physics Bulletin (DLCP-2026 special issue)
Primary Category: cs.LG
All Categories: cs.LG, astro-ph.IM

We compare feature-based machine learning and end-to-end deep learning for variable star classification using the Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF) StarEmbed dataset, which contains about 40,000 objects across seven imbalanced classes. Classical models trained on physically motivated features consistently outperform deep networks trained directly on raw light curves. We find that limited labeled data contributes to the performance gap, while the multivariate, irregularly sampled, and morphologically diverse nature of light curves poses additional challenges for deep learning models. While deep learning holds promise for learning representations directly from observations, our results indicate that current models fail to generalize with the available training set size. These findings motivate further investigation of the benefits of scaling labeled datasets and self-supervised pretraining on large unlabeled surveys. This work establishes a quantitative baseline for future research and highlights the need for further innovations to unlock the potential of deep learning in astronomical time series analysis.


Spin Spherical Fourier Neural Operators: Efficient Equivariant Learning for Vector and Spin Fields on the Sphere

Authors: Kevin Mulder, Matthew A. Price, Eirini Leftaki, Jason D. McEwen
Comments: 27 pages, 15 figures
Primary Category: cs.CV
All Categories: cs.CV, astro-ph.IM, cs.LG

Many fields of physical interest on the sphere, such as wind, the polarisation of the cosmic microwave background and the shear of weak gravitational lensing, are spin fields, whose values change phase when the local frame rotates. We extend spherical Fourier neural operators (SFNOs) to fields of any spin, through lifted spherical convolutions that are exactly rotationally equivariant. Lifting spin fields to Mackey functions on the rotation group provides the conceptual framework, while computation is performed exactly and efficiently through their harmonic representation and by appealing to sampling theorems on the sphere and rotation group. We construct spin SFNOs (S$^2$FNOs) from these building blocks. We demonstrate empirically that rotational equivariance holds to machine precision for these layers. We achieve state-of-the-art performance on existing benchmarks, and introduce new benchmarks on which spin-aware models outperform their scalar counterparts.


Unsupervised Neural Operators for Rotating Relativistic Stars: High-Fidelity Equation of State Inference and Pulsar Spin Evolution at Scale

Authors: Anarya Ray
Comments: No comment found
Primary Category: astro-ph.HE
All Categories: astro-ph.HE, cs.AI, cs.LG, gr-qc

Observed populations of rotating neutron stars~(NSs) with millisecond-order periods are powerful probes of fundamental physics. Mass-radius measurements inform poorly constrained properties of degenerate nuclear matter, whereas high-fidelity population synthesis connects unknowns in NS formation and binary evolution to large radio-timing surveys. However, repeated structure calculations are prohibitive due to the computational cost of solving the highly coupled partial differential equations of axisymmetric general relativistic hydrostatic equilibrium that govern rotating NS interiors. Non-rotating calculations or approximate rotational corrections can bias equation of state inference and limit the fidelity of population synthesis in tracing the evolutionary history of progenitors and propagating equation of state uncertainties. In this \textit{Letter}, we develop physics-informed neural operators that can solve the axisymmetric problem with high accuracy and negligible computational cost. \textit{Our solvers are trained in a fully unsupervised setting, with the training objective informed solely by the partial differential equations, and achieve a $10^3\times$ speed-up with negligible accuracy loss}. These solvers enable scalable EoS inference that self-consistently accounts for rotation and repeated stellar-structure calculations during binary evolution, making spin predictions that account for rotational structure and EoS uncertainties computationally accessible to population synthesis.


Boosted Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Solving Homogeneous and Non-Homogeneous Lane-Emden Type Equations

Authors: Luke Oluwaseye Joel
Comments: 24 pages, 25 figures
Primary Category: math-ph
All Categories: math-ph, astro-ph.GA, cs.LG, cs.NE

The Lane-Emden Equation is a class of nonlinear differential equations commonly used to model phenomena in astrophysics and mathematical physics, such as stellar structure and thermal behavior in polytropic gas spheres. This study presents a novel hybrid framework that integrates Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) with boosting methods to enhance the accuracy and robustness of solutions for Lane-Emden type differential equations. Standard PINNs leverage physical laws encoded within the loss function, but their predictive performance can be constrained by optimization challenges and approximation errors. To address this, we introduce a post-processing boosting stage where the residual errors from the PINN prediction are modeled using some regression algorithms - including LightGBM, Random Forest, XGBoost, and Support Vector Regression (SVR). The outputs from these boosted models are aggregated to generate a corrected, high-fidelity prediction. The proposed methodology is systematically validated through quantitative metrics and comparative analysis against the standard PINN baseline. The results demonstrate that the boosted PINN framework significantly reduces prediction error, and improves generalization, offering a powerful and flexible approach for solving scientific and engineering problems governed by differential equations.


Choosing a generator for streaming Gaussian noise in gravitational-wave mock data: accuracy, cost and reproducibility

Authors: Isaac C. F. Wong
Comments: 26 pages, 5 figures, 7 tables. Submitted to Classical and Quantum Gravity. Data: doi:10.5281/zenodo.23243286; reproduction code: https://github.com/isaac-cf-wong/noise-stitching-statistics-data
Primary Category: gr-qc
All Categories: gr-qc, astro-ph.IM, physics.data-an

Mock data challenges need long stretches of colored Gaussian noise with a prescribed spectrum. The candidates are the crossfade of independently drawn windows that the standard gravitational-wave libraries implement, overlap-save finite-impulse-response filtering, autoregressive and autoregressive moving-average (ARMA) models, and the reproducible filter of PyCBC. One measure ranks them all: the covariance error against the target over the analysis band, evaluated from each generator's algebra; generated noise only checks the implementations. We also measure throughput, retained state and the cost of bit-exact regeneration. With the target continued smoothly below the band, the crossfade with windows of 256 s or more reaches an error below $10^{-3}$ on all eleven detector curves as tabulated, at about 4.7 million samples per second from a state of a few hundred bytes. An overlap-save filter is faster but needs tens of thousands of taps for an error of a few percent, and no tested autoregressive or ARMA configuration lies on the accuracy--throughput fronts. An error of $10^{-3}$ needs a window of 8 to 235 s, longest on the design curves with narrow lines, where it also depends on the tabulation. With the target cut at the band edge, as current generators do by default, every method has an error above one half on the projected Advanced LIGO (aLIGO) O4 curve, and the crossfade on five other curves. The crossfade is cyclostationary. With the continued target, its cyclic part is the larger part of the error only at 8 s, on the aLIGO O4 and the Virgo and KAGRA design curves, and stays within a factor of two of the stationary part on the aLIGO O3 and the Virgo and KAGRA design curves. We recommend the crossfade with a window from the table of detector curves, and a smooth continuation of the target below the band.


Macrocanonical Generator Networks: Data-Efficient Neural Surrogates for Amortized Physics Simulation

Authors: Niall Jeffrey, Benjamin D. Wandelt
Comments: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. 20 pages total (inc. appendices), 4 figures
Primary Category: cs.LG
All Categories: cs.LG, astro-ph.CO, stat.ML

Many scientific simulations are computationally expensive, limiting their use in simulation-based inference, uncertainty quantification, and decision making. We introduce macrocanonical generator networks, a framework for learning fast, data-efficient neural surrogates for amortized simulation of stationary physical processes by matching multiscale statistics of a target process. Inspired by microcanonical maximum-entropy synthesis, which matches feature statistics per sample by inner-loop optimization, our approach instead trains a neural generator to satisfy the same constraints in expectation, preserving physically realistic sample-to-sample fluctuations. We prove that gradient-descent training induces preconditioned gradient-descent dynamics in sample space that inherit the symmetry-preservation properties of the microcanonical gradient descent of Bruna and Mallat, amortizing its per-sample inner-loop optimization into a single training run after which every sample is generated in one forward pass. We further show that residual generator parameterizations initialized near the identity, trained with a small learning rate, preserve entropy locally during early optimization and mitigate mode collapse. Experiments on cosmology and fluid simulations show that macrocanonical generators recover realistic samples from extremely small training sets, in some cases a single training example, while generating new realizations more than $10^6$ times faster per sample than the reference simulator and more than $10^3$ times faster than microcanonical gradient descent. The trained generators can be reused for downstream pretraining, including simulation-based inference, providing a data-efficient route to multi-fidelity scientific machine learning workflows.
