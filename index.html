Beyond the Kullback--Leibler divergence: the likelihood-ratio distribution as a detectability measure

Authors: Amirhossein Samandar, Mikel Martin Barandiaran, Linn Htat Lu, Andrew H. Jaffe, Deyan P. Mihaylov, Glenn D. Starkman, Cynthia Trendafilova, Yashar Akrami, Stefano Anselmi, Javier Carrón Duque, Craig J. Copi, Fernando Cornet-Gomez, Arthur Kosowsky, Anna Negro, Ricardo G. Rodrigues, Andrius Tamosiunas
Comments: 22 pages, 5 figures, 1 table
Primary Category: astro-ph.CO
All Categories: astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.IM, physics.data-an, stat.AP, stat.ME

The Kullback--Leibler (KL) divergence is the expectation value of the log-likelihood ratio between two specified models, evaluated with respect to one of the two models. It provides an average measure of the distinguishability of the models. We examine its connection to discovery potential and the accuracy of parameter estimates when one model represents a null hypothesis, and the alternative is a family of models characterized by one or more continuous parameters. For data drawn from a multivariate Gaussian distribution, we derive the log-likelihood-ratio distribution and its cumulants under two such hypotheses. We then search over the alternative model's parameter space and use peak prominence, the height of a likelihood-profile peak above its surrounding baseline, as an operational detection statistic. Repeating this search on simulated data drawn from the null model allows us to calibrate the prominence threshold for a specified false-positive rate. We study a toy Gaussian model as well as three cosmological examples involving cosmic topology, the tensor-to-scalar ratio, and dark energy. In all these cases, detectability of a particular model depends not only on KL divergence, but also on the size of the model parameter space, the likelihood-ratio distributions, and the distance of the model from any parameter space boundaries. The relationship between KL divergence, discovery potential, and parameter estimation must therefore be calibrated for each family of models and detection statistic under consideration.


Deep learning methods for gamma event selection in TAIGA-IACT image analysis in stereo-mode

Authors: E. O. Gres, A. P. Kryukov, P. A. Volchigov, A. P. Demichev, J. J. Dubenskaya, D. P. Zhurov, S. P. Polyakov, E. B. Postnikov, A. J. Razumov
Comments: 10 pages, 4 figures, Proceedings of The 10th International Conference in Deep Learning in Computational Physics, July 6-8, 2026, Moscow, Russia
Primary Category: astro-ph.IM
All Categories: astro-ph.IM, astro-ph.HE, cs.LG

In astrophysical complex TAIGA there are four Imaging Atmospheric Cherenkov telescopes (IACTs), located at a distance of 300-500 m from each other, due to which extensive air showers (EAS) generated by cosmic and gamma radiation are recorded by a single telescope or by multiple telescopes simultaneously (stereo-mode). The analysis of EAS images from two or more Cherenkov telescopes together is of particular interest, since for this approach the reconstruction of physical parameters of the primary gamma ray is much more accurate than in mono-observations. However, the gamma-ray event reconstruction strongly depends on the quality of the hadron background suppression of the classifier, therefore, the gamma event selection stage is one of the important ones in the further analysis of IACT data. We propose and study several neural network methods for gamma event selection in joint TAIGA-IACT image analysis. Neural network methods we suggested involves a single-stage analysis of IACT images from two telescopes, while the second method considers the formation of autoencoder-derived essential features that contain the EAS physical parameters, including the type of primary particle.


$\texttt{NFDiag}$: High-dimensional MCMC Convergence Diagnosis with Normalizing Flows

Authors: Kutay Nazlı, Elena Sellentin
Comments: No comment found
Primary Category: stat.ML
All Categories: stat.ML, astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.IM, cs.LG

Accurately assessing the convergence of Monte Carlo Markov Chains is crucial for ensuring their validity in Bayesian inference problems. However, convergence assessment in high dimensions is known to be difficult, due to most convergence diagnostics becoming insensitive beyond a few dozen parameters. Conventional convergence statistics analyze marginal distributions which in high dimensions neglects increasing levels of information from the joint and conditional probability distributions. Thus we propose $\texttt{NFDiag}$, which taps into the information of the full multivariate joint posterior with a normalizing flow (NF). Our convergence diagnostic thereby also analyzes information from conditional distributions. In other words, by working in the full multivariate space, it is sensitive with respect to the geometry of the chain in high dimensions. Our diagnostic utilizes that NFs assume independent and identically distributed (iid) training samples, which is a testable assumption that will be violated by a non-converged or strongly correlated chain. Our diagnostic trains NFs on separate parts of a chain, and then assesses whether these correspond to the same multivariate target distribution. We test our diagnostic in up to 250 dimensions, and demonstrate it is universal and unbiased, and detects problematic chain quality also when Gelman-Rubin $\hat{R}$ and the multivarite effective sample size do not.


Missed Detections and False Alarms in a CNN Technosignature Detector on Breakthrough Listen Data

Authors: Omer Imran
Comments: 17 pages, 14 figures. Code and data: https://github.com/OmerImran/seti-two-directional-failure
Primary Category: astro-ph.IM
All Categories: astro-ph.IM, cs.LG, physics.pop-ph

Radio technosignature searches rely on machine learning detectors to shrink the candidate pool before a human looks at it. They are validated on held-out data from the training distribution, which excludes the cases where they are most likely to fail: a technosignature has unknown morphology, and terrestrial interference is unbounded in variety. I trained a standard competition detector once, froze it, and probed it with 15,000 injections across ten morphologies and five amplitudes, placed into real Green Bank backgrounds. Two directions were tested: genuine signals with morphologies other than a straight narrowband line, and interference resembling the training data. Recall did not follow the predicted ordering, and it fell as signals brightened, even for a needle of the kind the detector was trained on (from 0.590 to 0.090). One interference morphology reached a false positive rate of 0.627, comparable to the detector's own baseline recall of 0.5525 on genuine signals. Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) showed attention along the injected path in one missed family by a pre-specified test, and visually in a second. A pre-specified, time-averaged ON-OFF filter removed false alarms and genuine detections at the same rate. The detector reads only the ON panels. Since the only evidence distinguishing a genuine candidate from terrestrial interference lies in the discarded OFF panels, it cannot tell if a signal originates from the target star; it can only tell if it resembles the training data. This explains the false alarm directly; the missed signals reflect what the detector learned to recognise, which reading the OFF panels would not by itself change.
