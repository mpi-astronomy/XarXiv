JEPA for astronomy: multi-survey spectral alignment puts stellar parameters on the leading principal axes

Authors: Xiaosheng Zhao, Rosemary F. G. Wyse, Yuan-Sen Ting, Alexander S. Szalay
Comments: 15 pages, 5 figures, 2 tables. Submitted to AAS journals. Comments welcome
Primary Category: astro-ph.GA
All Categories: astro-ph.GA, astro-ph.IM, astro-ph.SR, cs.LG

Spectra from different surveys carry different systematics and are not calibrated against one another. Multi-survey foundation models for stellar spectra are then built by aligning embeddings of the same star observed across surveys. Most in astronomy follow the recipes established for language and vision, aligning with a contrastive loss that separates every star from the others. However, this does not ensure that the stellar physics is encoded in a form that a simple read-out can recover. Holding the cross-survey architecture fixed and varying only the alignment loss, we measure what an objective encodes from the principal-component geometry of the Gaia BP/RP (XP) spectra and from a linear probe of the stellar parameters. In a properly aligned embedding, the leading two principal components form a plane that recovers an analog of the Kiel diagram, the third component tracks [Fe/H], and [alpha/Fe] varies coherently across the plane, such that the chemically defined Galactic disks and accreted populations occupy distinct loci. We show that legibility, the ease of reading the information out, is highest for a training objective that never pushes stars apart, and instead constrains only the overall distribution of each survey's embeddings (JEPA). JEPA also exceeds the contrastive alternative at the same architecture, and the performance improves further as more surveys (Gaia XP+LAMOST LRS+DESI+APOGEE) are included. Predicted abundances for 8.0 million XP giants reproduce the main [alpha/Fe]-[Fe/H] structure of the Milky Way. These results indicate that the choice of alignment objective, not alignment alone, determines whether stellar labels are linearly recoverable.


A COMPASS to the Galaxy: Joint nucleosynthetic yield set comparison and parameter inference in Galactic chemical evolution

Authors: Berkay Günes, Giuseppe Viterbo, Sven Buder, Tobias Buck
Comments: No comment found
Primary Category: astro-ph.GA
All Categories: astro-ph.GA, astro-ph.IM, cs.LG, physics.data-an

Galactic Chemical Evolution (GCE) models connect stellar nucleosynthesis to observed abundance patterns, but their interpretation requires simultaneous parameter inference and comparison between competing stellar yield prescriptions. This remains challenging due to intractable likelihoods and growing dataset complexity. Using compass, a novel simulation-based inference framework, we perform, for the first time, amortized joint Bayesian model comparison and parameter inference for GCE models with intractable likelihoods within a unified architecture. Compass employs a conditional score-based diffusion model with a transformer architecture to learn both the posterior and likelihood directly from forward simulations, enabling efficient inference and likelihood-based ranking of competing nucleosynthetic yield sets. In controlled tests, compass identifies the generating yield set and recovers ground-truth parameters with accuracy comparable to existing simulation-based inference (SBI) methods, while reducing computational cost by orders of magnitude. Applied to Milky Way data, the framework favors a hybrid NuGrid + IllustrisTNG yield prescription, confirmed by an independent classifier, and infers an initial mass function slope of $α_{\rm IMF} = -2.65 \pm 0.02$ and a SN$_{\rm Ia}$ normalization of $\log(N_{\rm Ia}) = -2.81 \pm 0.02$, with a systematic spread of $\approx 0.3$ in $α_{\rm IMF}$ between the best-ranked yield sets, consistent with independent constraints. The attention mechanism confirms that the inferred parameters are driven by physically meaningful abundance signatures. By unifying amortized parameter inference, Bayesian model comparison, and interpretability in a single probabilistic framework, compass provides a scalable approach for precision chemical evolution studies with current and forthcoming spectroscopic surveys.


Improving Early Detection of Scientifically Interesting Radio Sources Using Enhanced Isolation Forest

Authors: Pfunzo Thavhana, Clement Nyirenda, Michelle Lochner
Comments: 7 pages, 6 figures
Primary Category: astro-ph.IM
All Categories: astro-ph.IM, cs.LG

The rapid growth of radio astronomical surveys has created a major challenge in identifying scientifically interesting sources from large populations of observed objects. This study investigates whether enhanced Isolation Forest (iForest) methods can improve the early detection and ranking of scientifically interesting radio sources. Four iForest variants, Standard Enhanced, Pooled Gain, Kurtosis-based, and Hypervolume, are evaluated using 1,031 extended radio sources from the MeerKAT Galaxy Cluster Legacy Survey (MGCLS). Each source is represented by a 52-dimensional feature set derived using Bootstrap Your Own Latent (BYOL) self-supervised learning and principal component analysis (PCA), while expert scientific-interest ratings are used to define the ground-truth labels. The methods are evaluated using Average Precision (AP), receiver operating characteristic area under the curve (ROC AUC), Precision@10, Precision@50, and cumulative anomaly detection. Pooled Gain achieves the highest Average Precision (AP = 0.201) and the strongest early-stage ranking performance (P@10 = 0.600), identifying six expert-validated anomalies among the ten highest-ranked sources. Standard Enhanced and Kurtosis-based achieve the highest ROC AUC (0.677), while the AUC values of the four variants range from 0.653 to 0.677. These results indicate that feature-aware isolation strategies can improve the prioritization of scientifically interesting sources at the top of the ranked list, although overall discrimination remains comparable across the evaluated variants. The findings support the use of enhanced Isolation Forest methods as an initial ranking mechanism within human-in-the-loop systems for large-scale radio astronomical surveys.


Searching the Pareto Front: Multi-Objective Retrieval for Heterogeneous Scientific Modalities

Authors: Song Young Oh, Nesar Ramachandra, Bin Xia, Ian Foster, Kyle Chard
Comments: No comment found
Primary Category: cs.AI
All Categories: cs.AI, astro-ph.IM

In cosmology, surveys span different sky regions and wavelength ranges, resulting in uneven modality coverage across observational datasets. Such heterogeneity can be addressed through either multimodal foundation models (FMs), which learn shared embedding spaces, or independently learned modality embeddings combined via late fusion. We investigate whether the latter provides an adaptive alternative to FM-based scientific retrieval. We evaluate this approach on a galaxy retrieval task spanning diverse modalities--history of star formation, spectra of light emitted, and multi-wavelength observations from two telescopes--comparing an FM baseline with separately trained embeddings fused through multi-objective optimization based on Pareto optimality. We find that the FM baseline achieves strong embedding-space proximity, but our method preserves modality-specific distance structure that allows scientists to inspect which physical signals drive similarity. Our method also achieves improved fidelity in raw-data space: across six measured properties, its nearest neighbor is 8% closer to the query on average than that retrieved by kNN in the FM embedding space (spread 0.128 vs. 0.139). These results suggest that multi-objective retrieval can surface astrophysically coherent candidates that single-objective search alone may overlook.


Surrogate Modeling and Reduced-Order Methods in Gravitational-Wave Physics and Numerical Relativity: From Spectral Representations to Adaptive Compression

Authors: Manuel Tiglio
Comments: No comment found
Primary Category: gr-qc
All Categories: gr-qc, astro-ph.HE, cs.LG, physics.comp-ph

The rapid expansion of gravitational-wave astronomy and high-precision numerical relativity has created an acute computational bottleneck: direct numerical integration of the Einstein field equations and high-dimensional Bayesian parameter estimation require millions of waveform evaluations that cannot be sustained by traditional solvers alone. Reduced-order modeling (ROM) and surrogate techniques have emerged as foundational tools to bridge this gap, compressing high-fidelity solution manifolds into compact, rapidly evaluable representations without sacrificing physical accuracy. This perspective reviews the mathematical pillars of projection-based reduced-order modeling---including Proper Orthogonal Decomposition, greedy basis selection, and the Empirical Interpolation Method---and traces their operational impact in gravitational-wave data analysis through surrogate waveform families and Reduced-Order Quadratures. We then examine the fundamental approximation barriers posed by slow Kolmogorov n-width decay in non-stationary, transient, and strongly nonlinear regimes, showing how adaptive hp-greedy domain partitioning and multi-scale spectral compression overcome these limits. Finally, we discuss how combining certified reduced-order structures with modern scientific machine learning and sensitivity-aware quantization provides a rigorous blueprint for next-generation detectors such as LISA, Einstein Telescope, and Cosmic Explorer.


automodel: A Skill for Agentic Model Discovery

Authors: Marco Virgolin, Harry Desmond, Ester Bergantin, Giorgio Morales, John W. Sheppard, Luigi Romano, Alessandro Lucantonio
Comments: No comment found
Primary Category: cs.AI
All Categories: cs.AI, astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.GA, cs.NE

The search for better models, whether expressed as analytical equations or neural architectures, has increasingly shifted from manual design toward data-driven automation. While a multitude of algorithms exist, ranging from exhaustive search to AlphaEvolve, recent advances in general-purpose coding agents such as Claude Code and OpenAI Codex raise the question: could providing such systems with a "model discovery recipe" be enough to discover or improve models? We present automodel, an agent skill for general model discovery. With case studies in astrophysics, cardiovascular dynamics, precision agriculture, traffic flow, and vehicle tire dynamics, we show how commercial agents equipped with automodel can successfully identify novel and scientifically-relevant models. Our findings suggest that encoding model-discovery methodology as a portable agent skill can provide a simple, interactive, powerful and broadly applicable alternative to domain-specific automated discovery pipelines. The skill is available at: https://github.com/unlayer-ai/automodel.
