Is the learner or the summary the limit? Auditing machine-learning energy reconstruction for air-shower arrays on KASCADE and GRAPES-3

Authors: Mugdha Raje Ashiya, Samarjeet Malik
Comments: 26 pages, 14 figures, 14 tables. Submitted to Eur. Phys. J. C. Code: https://github.com/SamarjeetMalik/summary-representation-limits
Primary Category: astro-ph.HE
All Categories: astro-ph.HE, astro-ph.IM, cs.LG, hep-ex, physics.data-an

Machine-learning energy estimators for cosmic-ray arrays are usually trained on a few fitted shower parameters, not station signals. When their resolution stops improving, standard diagnostics cannot say whether a better model would help or the summary has run out of information. We use a five-step audit that compares trained models with a learner-independent estimate of the lowest error the summary allows (its squared-loss Bayes risk, or floor), tests which inputs add information and flags inputs merely computed from the fit. We apply it to public KASCADE simulations with three hadronic-interaction models (QGSJET-II-04, EPOS-LHC and SIBYLL-2.3d), to 13,848,509 measured KASCADE events and to simulated GRAPES-3 events. Within known energy decades below 10^16 eV, gradient boosting on nine reconstructed features is within 2.2% of the estimated floor under every estimator setting, so more capacity is not expected to gain more than about 2%. The floor is an estimate, accurate to about 1% in a semi-synthetic benchmark with intervals that under-cover, and not a bound on the true Bayes risk. Replacing reconstructed by true particle numbers removes 70 to 72% of the error variance below 10^15 eV and 24 to 26% between 10^16 and 10^17 eV on all three models, yet simulated station signals close about a tenth of that gap. On GRAPES-3, networks given images rendered from the fit do no better than the fit. In measured data, the mean muon size at fixed electron size lies within every model's proton-to-iron bracket and a label-free probe finds the muon channel used as in simulation; neither fixes the energy scale. Energy-labelled results are simulated.


Wavelet-CFM for the extragalactic millimeter-wave sky

Authors: M. Mebratu, W. L. K. Wu, S. Gasiorowski
Comments: 10 pages, 3 figures, 1 table. Accepted to the 9th Machine Learning and the Physical Sciences Workshop (ML4PS)
Primary Category: astro-ph.CO
All Categories: astro-ph.CO, cs.LG

We develop a Wavelet-factorized Conditional Flow Matching (Wavelet-CFM) framework to generate fast, accurate simulations of correlated astrophysical fields including gravitational lensing convergence ($κ$), the kinetic and thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (kSZ and tSZ) effects, and cosmic infrared background (CIB) emission. Together these make up every bright extragalactic component of the millimeter-wave (mm-wave) sky apart from radio sources. Our approach combines multi-scale wavelet decomposition with conditional flow matching models, trained on $\sim$9$\times$9 deg$^2$ patches at 1-arcminute resolution. The four fields are generated jointly, including their cross couplings, and the model has a tractable likelihood, which to our knowledge has not previously been achieved for this set of components. The power spectra, one-point distributions and Minkowski functionals of the generated maps match a validation set to a few percent. These results show promise for enabling field-level cosmological analyses of mm-wave data.


World Models Are the Right Foundation for Simulating Mars Terraforming

Authors: Sameera S Kashyap, Akshitha Nagaraj, Razvan Marinescu
Comments: No comment found
Primary Category: cs.LG
All Categories: cs.LG, astro-ph.EP, astro-ph.IM

Terraforming Mars is a long-horizon, action-conditioned control problem that requires predicting atmospheric responses to interventions over centuries to identify effective policies. This position paper argues that physics-regularized recurrent state-space world models are the most promising foundation for this setting. Data-driven climate emulators lack action-conditioning, and physics-based general circulation models (GCMs) are too expensive for policy search. Among learned world models, recurrent state-space architectures with physics constraints are better suited than transformer-based alternatives: they provide native uncertainty quantification through their stochastic-deterministic decomposition, remain stable under sparse non-terrestrial training data, and enforce physical consistency at each rollout step. To make this position concrete, we describe a potential architecture combining JEPA-inspired latent encoding, universal differential equation constraints, and a Dreamer-lineage planning head. We also introduce MarsWorld, an open benchmark proposal for intervention-conditioned planetary world models, and discuss broader implications and objections.


On the distribution function for cross-correlation estimators in cosmology and beyond

Authors: Robert N. Cahn, Marc Kamionkowski
Comments: 7 pages
Primary Category: astro-ph.CO
All Categories: astro-ph.CO, physics.data-an

The ubiquitous Gaussian distribution, $e^{-x^2/2}/\sqrt{2π}$, has mean of zero and variance of 1. It is well known that the estimator $\widehat{x^2} = N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N x_i^2$ for the variance obtained from $N$ samples $x_i$ is drawn from a $χ_n^2$ distribution, a result used often to assess the implications of data for a model. One often also needs to measure the covariance, or cross-correlation, of two different variables $x$ and $y$. If the distributions for $x$ and $y$ are both gaussian but independent of each other, the estimator $N^{-1}\sum_i x_i y_i$ for the cross-correlation is distributed about its expectation value (zero) with variance unity. The distribution function for this estimator may also be required to interpret data, for example in the measurement of cosmological cross-correlations. This distribution---the cross-correlation analog of the $χ_n^2$ distribution---appears in the literature, but it has not been widely internalized by many in the field. We therefore here present two pedagogical derivations of this distribution and discuss some of its properties. We discuss in particular the connection to cosmological observables, but note several other areas of physics where these results may apply.


Machine learning enables full Bayesian inference for spinning extreme-mass-ratio inspirals

Authors: Bo Liang, Minghui Du, Zhenwei Lyu, Hanlin Song, Jiakai Zhang, Weichen Qin, Chia-Jui Chou, Yi Yang, Jingyi Yu, Ziren Luo, Mingming Sun, Peng Xu, Yue-Liang Wu
Comments: 22 pages, 5 figures, 1 table; Extended Data: 4 figures, 2 tables
Primary Category: gr-qc
All Categories: gr-qc, astro-ph.HE, astro-ph.IM, cs.AI

Extreme-mass-ratio inspirals (EMRIs), stellar-mass compact objects spiralling into massive black holes, promise to measure the masses and spins of the central black holes, reveal the strong-field environment around them and gauge cosmic distances via millihertz gravitational-wave observations. Yet most forecasts for the parameter inference of these systems assume known source parameters, and broad-prior analyses have addressed only simplified versions of the problem. The joint inversion of all 14 parameters of a spinning source from the complete prior has remained unsolved. Here we solve it for the augmented analytic kludge model: generative networks narrow the complete prior and reveal a ring of degenerate spin directions, and the exact detector likelihood determines the posterior. For a simulated one-year LISA-Taiji observation, within one ring sector we obtain, for the first time, a 14-parameter posterior of a spinning EMRI from the complete prior, the most precise yet obtained for such a source from broad priors. The redshifted mass and spin of the massive black hole are determined to two parts in $10^5$, the mass two orders of magnitude beyond any electromagnetic measurement. The compact object's redshifted mass is measured to one part in $10^4$, and its eccentric, retrograde orbit discriminates between capture channels. The 0.8% distance and sub-square-degree localization are sufficient for dark-siren cosmology. Year-long coherent analysis resolves the inspiral driven by radiation reaction itself.
